VainSabers temporal motion blur for Beat Saber 1.40.8

The previous renderer moved individual vertices through the pose history. On some swings, that folded the transparent mesh across itself. It could also leave a dark band next to the current blade, especially on low-sided meshes.

The replacement uses a separate camera-facing strip for each moving part. Each cross-section widens to cover the visible sideways movement. The renderer reads the pose history and stops at the first significant reversal in the projected motion. The fragment shader computes when the moving cross-section enters and leaves each pixel, then integrates a smooth shutter over that interval. Opacity drops toward the oldest edge and the two ends of the strip. The strip can reach the current blade independently of the number of sides on the original mesh.

Tube blades, sprites, and OBJ parts use the same exposure calculation. The original mesh and textures render at rest and fade as the lateral sweep takes over. OBJ strips use the mesh bounds and broad albedo. Movement mostly in depth keeps the source mesh visible. The mod still uses eight stored poses, the existing rendering passes, and a maximum exposure setting of 25 ms. The dedicated menu laser and standard ribbon trail retain their existing renderers.

The implementation approximates the three-dimensional trajectory with continuous motion along its projected interval. OBJ cross-sections come from the model bounds.

The exposure now has a soft core and a wider halo. Both come from analytical filters of the same shutter integral. The filter widths grow with visible lateral travel, which softens the edges of a wide swing without adding a scene texture lookup or another rendering pass.

Unity tests on Direct3D 11 covered fast movement, an oblique reversal, a depth swing, and resting geometry. I tested the filtered exposure with vain-ring and vain-seg-inv at Quality 1.0 and the maximum 1.5. In a 900p test frame, the leading fade on vain-ring widened from 2 to 92 pixels. The stationary and depth-swing frames matched the unfiltered version. The earlier integration tests also covered the embedded Wii Remote OBJ and texture, a sprite, and the menu pointer preset. Headset rendering and VR frame timings still need testing.

The main implementation is in TemporalExposure.cginc. BlurPart.cginc connects it to the lit and unlit saber shaders, and BlurTube.cs creates the exposure strips. In the asset creation project, rebuild vs_assets with Tools > Build AssetBundles in Unity before rebuilding VainSabers.dll.
